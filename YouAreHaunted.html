You Are Haunted

An abandoned castle, two ghosts called Tony and Barry, and a way out you have to earn.

Play the game My process
Overview

You are never alone in this castle

You Are Haunted is a VR horror game created in Unreal Engine 5. The game takes place in a dilapidated castle from which the player must try to escape. Roaming the castle are the spirits of two brothers who once lived there with their father.

Chapter 01 · Design phase

Prototype

Before the castle looked like a castle, we blocked it out with simple shapes to test the layout, the scale and the route.

Blockout & Map

We started with a sketch of the castle map and built it in Unreal Engine with simple blocks. This let me test the size of the rooms, the length of the corridors and the route from the start to the exit before anything was decorated. In VR, scale matters a lot: a room that looks fine on a monitor can feel huge or cramped when you stand in it. When I opened the game to capture additional videos and screenshots, I discovered that all the asset files were gone.

VR Setup

Before building the real game, I set up the VR player and tested it. I wanted the basics, like moving around and grabbing objects, to feel comfortable first. Everything else in the game depends on that. For this project, we used the Oculus Quest 2. We downloaded the Meta Horizon Link application to run the game on the headset. Additionally, we requested longer cables to ensure sufficient room for movement while using the headset.

Chapter 02 · Interaction

Interactables

In VR the player uses their hands, so every object has to be easy to understand. All videos on this page are vertical because of VR

Lever

The lever is a puzzle element that the player must pull using their hand. I've implemented this within a 'Blueprint' that tracks whether the player has touched the lever and triggers the next event once the end position is reached. I wanted the player to operate the lever physically, as that feels much more satisfying in VR than simply pressing a button.

Paper

The player needed to feel immersed in the game. I chose to convey the idea that an explorer had been there before you, leaving behind notes that could guide you toward the exit. These papers can be picked up and carried with you throughout the level. The papers tell the story of the explorer who came before you. He also created a map of the secret passage to Barry's room.

Door

The door opens when the player throws something against it. I wanted the player to think about what they can do in VR instead of just walking up to a door. The blueprint checks what hits the door and opens it when the hit is hard enough.

Closet

The player can grab the closet and pull it to the side. I made this so players who pay attention to their surroundings are rewarded with a new path. The closet slides to the side instead of flying around, so it stays controlled in VR. For this closet, I also considered how to direct the player's attention. To achieve this, I used light and blood splatters. During playtesting, I observed that once these elements were added, it was usually immediately clear to the player where they needed to go.

Chapter 03 · Level design

Level & enemy

The map and the enemy have to work together, so I made sure both could only do what I wanted them to do.

Stair

I used barriers on the stairs so the player can walk up and down them smoothly. My duo partner used a navmesh bounds for the enemies. During different playtests we found out that the enemy could not walk there, so I solved it with the barriers as well.

Enemy Blockout

The enemy could walk and stand almost anywhere, which made it look broken. With this blockout I limited the area where the enemy can go, so it only moves where it makes sense and always ends up somewhere fair for the player. This also gives the player more freedom regarding where they can stand and move, preventing enemies from reaching them and launching an attack.

Bookshelf Maze

A maze made of bookshelves. The narrow paths make the player feel lost and tense, since the enemy can be around any corner. It is a different feeling than the big halls in the rest of the castle. I also made use of sound effects and visual environmental scares here to frighten the player.

Chapter 04 · Horror

Scares

The castle should never feel safe, so I added scares in the environment and in the story.

Crow

The crow is an environmental scare. When the player walks underneath it, the statue falls to the ground. Nothing really happens to the player, but it makes them wonder what else in the castle might move. The environmental objects that move help the game create a greater sense of fear for the player. To achieve this, I used a barrier that tracks the player's location, combined with a 'move to' component.

Environmental scares

Besides the crow I added more small things that happen around the player. They are not dangerous, but they keep the player tense and make the castle feel alive. The environmental sounds associated with objects help the game by drawing the enemy toward those sounds; this alerts the player to be on their guard, as the enemy may be approaching or already nearby. In the example video, a candlestick can be heard falling over, to which the enemy immediately reacts.

Scare

This is one of the main scares, which combines visuals and sound at a chosen moment. After the quiet parts of the castle, the player is not expecting it, and that is what makes it work. The best way to keep the player's attention focused on the game and to induce fear is through the use of jumpscares. I designed these scares based on the narrative: they feature the father of the two brothers, who is still somewhere inside the castle. He is not one of the game's main enemies and cannot harm the player—his only purpose is to startle them. I implemented this using a barrier and a blueprint that triggers a sound at a specific location while making the mesh visible for a split second.

Ending Sequence

When the player escapes, a short ending sequence plays. I built it so the game closes the story in a clear way, instead of just stopping when the player reaches the exit. The ending sequence was created using two cameras and audio, and it utilizes the level I designed. Although it isn't visible in the image since all the meshes have been removed the sequence (cutscene) made it immediately clear to the player that they had escaped the castle: you crawl through a tunnel, and then, out in the open air, the message "You escaped" appears.

Chapter 05 · Sound design

Sound

In VR you can hear where things are, so sound is one of the best ways to build tension.

Ambience

I added ambient sounds like wind and quiet background noises so the castle feels empty and old. The goal was to make the player feel alone, but never completely safe.

Squeaks

Creaking and squeaking sounds make the old castle feel real. They also make the player look around, because they never know what caused the sound. These images are used to send one to the player.

Chapter T · Testing

Playtests

We let others play the game several times. Watching them showed us things we never noticed ourselves.

Playtesting

“Why do we have a green wall?” — “I don't know.”

We conducted numerous playtests with students, teachers, and also at home with friends and family. We were successful in conveying a sense of tension within the game, with the VR component playing a significant role in this; however, we also discovered that people can quickly become nauseous when playing VR games. The playtest we conducted helped us improve the game.

Build problem

At the end of the project, we had to build the game and upload it to itch.io. Building the Unreal project took 4.5 hours on my laptop. The build worked, but many assets were missing, as shown here.

Iteration

The haunted ending

After eight weeks of working on You Are Haunted, I am really proud of what we made. As a team of two, we wanted a VR horror game where the player never feels safe, and I think we did that. The game was chosen as the best game of the semester, which was a great moment after all the work. The playtests taught me the most, because they showed what was actually scary and what was only confusing. Working in VR made me think differently about space, sound and what the player does with their hands.

Creating a game in virtual reality is not easy. There are many factors to consider. Players can move their camera anywhere and walk into areas where you don't want them to go. During the development process, I also found it quite frustrating that I had to put on the headset every time I wanted to test something it would quickly make me feel dizzy.

I learned a lot about game design during this project.

  • Designing a level for VR, where scale and comfort matter
  • Using sound to guide and scare the player
  • Fixing problems that only show up in playtests
  • Working together as a team of two in Unreal Engine 5
