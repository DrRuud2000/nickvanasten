Sublevel

An abandoned bunker horror game about exploring, surviving and never feeling quite safe.

Play the game My process
Overview

One bunker. One way out.

Sublevel is a horror game set in an abandoned underground bunker. You wake up alone and have to find a way to escape before the scary creatures find you. It was built in Unreal Engine 5 by a team of three in eight weeks. (Semester 2, Game Development)

Chapter 01 · Design phase

Prototype

Before anything looked good, we blocked out the bunker with simple shapes to test layout and pacing.

Core Idea

Before we had a clear idea of what we were going to create, we researched various horror games. We then began roughing out our concept on a whiteboard and drafted an initial sketch of the map. After discussing the idea with several instructors, we realized that our scope was too ambitious; consequently, we removed elements such as cutscenes and the "turn on the power" mechanic from the final product.

Prototype Scare

Before the three of us began working on the actual game, we each created our own prototype. I took charge of the player-related elements, developing a tension-building scare mechanic, a hiding spot, and player movement.

Chapter 02 · Movement

Player

For Sublevel, I designed the player by using a player character and blueprints.

Movement

The movement in the game needed to avoid feeling too fast, as the player should feel a sense of fear. We arrived at this conclusion after several playtests, aiming to find the perfect balance of movement speed and stamina for our player within a horror environment.

Camera

Regarding the camera effect, I focused specifically on making the first-person perspective feel as authentic as possible. To achieve this, I implemented a head-bob feature. Through various playtests and feedback sessions, we fine-tuned the speed and intensity of the head-bob to ensure the best possible player experience as the character's head moves in-game.

Flashlight

The bunker is dark to create a sense of abandonment and heighten tension for the player. Nevertheless, we decided to provide the first-person player with a flashlight. I designed this flashlight. Once the player picks it up, they keep it with them at all times—even if they die. The player is also given the option not to pick up the flashlight, or to turn the flashlight off.

Chapter 03 · Interaction

Interactables

Everything the player can touch runs on one unified Blueprint interaction system, so new interactables could be added quickly.

Linetrace

For the interactables, I opted to use a line-trace function—one that remains invisible during gameplay while still being fully functional. I also enlarged the hitboxes for these interactables, making it easier for the player to engage with the objects. Since I was also responsible for the audio, I incorporated sound effects and certain movements to further immerse the player in the game.

Door

We didn't want to make it too easy for the player to escape the bunker, so I added a keycard and a door that work in tandem. The door and the keycard are also placed far apart to ensure the escape isn’t too simple. Following several playtests, I made it easier for the player to locate the keycard by using light and various objects to help guide them toward it.

Valve

At the beginning, it appears that there is only one direction to go. The player also encounters a flooded room containing an enemy. I have created a guide on how the player can drain the room to progress with the story. Furthermore, through the use of sound effects and audio cues, I have made it clear that the enemy has escaped from the room, thereby opening up a path.

Locker (prototype)

During the prototyping phase and the initial conceptual stage of Sublevel, I created hiding spots. These were intended to allow the player to conceal themselves and evade enemies (similar to The Outlast Trials). While this feature is present in Sublevel, it is not a core mechanic and remains unfinished. Following several playtests and feedback sessions, we realized that we lacked the time required to fully complete this feature.

Chapter 04 · Sound design

Atmosphere

My main focus was to add ambient sounds and to scare the player through visuals and sound.

Ambience

For this game, I needed to create the impression that the player is inside an abandoned bunker. To achieve this, I incorporated sounds like those of a radiator and faint, distant dripping—subtle background audio designed to make the player feel truly immersed in the environment. The goal was to guide the player through the level without providing too many explicit hints. During the showcase, I received many compliments on my sound design.

Jumpscare

My task was to scare the player using sounds and visuals. To achieve this, I created a "Jefrey_Jumpscare." In this sequence, a character appears on the player's camera, the lights go out, and the player's view is locked, forcing them to look straight ahead. Many people were truly startled by this fantastic jumpscare! : )

Chapter 05 · UI

Widgets

User interface that guides the player through the bunker and provides information

UI Indicators

I had to guide the player and provide information as they moved through the bunker. To do this, I created a widget system that displays information to the player, triggered at specific moments. In this example, you can see the time the player needs to survive.

Interaction prompts

I have checked all the interactables and looked at what the player might do wrong. If the player does not follow the story in the correct way, they will soon be made aware of this through a widget.

Game Explanation

Before the player enters the bunker, I have opted to include a loading screen that displays all the controls, ensuring the player knows in advance what actions they can take when facing danger. This loading screen also serves to initiate the main level.

Iteration

The end of a new beginning

After the eight weeks and this project, I was very pleased with the final result. As a team, we had given our all to make Sublevel a great game and to ensure it truly felt like a horror experience. During the showcase event, we received many compliments from fellow students and lecturers; people hadn't expected the game to look so good. We also witnessed plenty of jump scares from those who played it. After working on this game, it became immediately clear to me that I want to create games rather than just play them.

I learned a lot about game design during this project.

  • Making choices regarding the addition and omission of mechanics
  • How best to incorporate feedback from others into the game
  • Using Unreal Engine
  • Creating a game in which the player feels tension
