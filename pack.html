Chapter 01 · Game design

Why I design games

I don't just want to play games, I want to make them. Pack is where that shows the most.

What I enjoy

What I like most about game design is working out how a player will feel. I think about what they see, what they do and what they fear, and then I build the game around that. I also enjoy development itself: solving problems, testing, and seeing an idea slowly become something you can actually play.

I have a big imagination, and you can see that in every project in this portfolio. Sublevel is a bunker full of tension, Splitstar is a world with its own rules, Tower Clash is a battle, and the VR Horror Castle puts you inside the story. Pack is the most personal one, because everything in it, from the rooms to the host, comes from my own head.

Chapter 02 · Narrative

Why storytelling

A good mechanic is fun for a minute. A good story makes the player care about it.

From idea to story

For Pack I started with a simple question: who would build a place like this, and why? The answer became the host. His obsession with games began with family tragedies tied to games of Ludo and Bully, and that explains why he turns games into dangerous spectacles.

Because the host has a past, every room means something. The puzzles are not random, they are part of his show. That is what I want to do as a designer: tell a story through the game itself, so the player discovers it instead of just reading it.

Chapter 03 · Character

The Pack

The Pack is the mastermind behind the facility. He is the voice in your ear, on the screens and sometimes even on a hoverboard.

When you fail

When you lose a challenge, you respawn at the start of that room and The Pack reacts. Sometimes he is angry, sometimes he finds it hilarious, and sometimes he is almost sad that you failed. Turn the sound on to hear what he says.

Failing is part of the game. You can try a different approach without losing all your progress, and his comments make every retry feel personal.

Angry, but amused

He gets mad when you fail, but he also enjoys the show. Often both at the same time.

Quick to get attached

He grows fond of his participants fast, and he doesn't give up on them easily.

In your head

He loves torturing his players and messing with their minds. That is what makes him charming and hateful.

Chapter 04 · Level design

Room by room

Every locked room has its own puzzle, trap or test, and the challenges get harder the deeper you go.

Dark comedy

The facility is built from stark, box-like rooms with purple lights and subtle circus details. The look is unsettling like a game show, and it mixes real tension with The Pack's absurd, cruel humour.

I wanted the player to feel toyed with instead of just tested. Study the room, take a risk and figure out how it works before it gets the better of you.

Chapter 05 · Worldbuilding

Little helpers

The Pack doesn't build his show alone.

The robot minions

Small robots work as his henchmen. They prepare and maintain the rooms so the show can always continue. They also drive the story: one room is still under construction, and that is your chance to slip into the hidden corridors, break out of the challenge loop and find out what The Pack is really doing.

Iteration

Escape the show

The Pack promises a cash prize, but the deeper you go, the more the game becomes about taking back control. Will you play by his rules, or use the unfinished room to find a way out? Pack is still an idea, but it shows how I think as a designer: I start with a feeling, build a world and a character around it, and then turn it into something a player can experience.

What Pack shows about me as a game designer.

  • Turning a big idea into a clear game concept
  • Building a character that drives the story
  • Balancing tension and humour
  • Keeping failure fun so players want to try again
